# Summary: Belief Check on an AI tool for dental insurance claims

19 Nov 2026

The question: Small US dental practices lose hours each week on insurance claims. Would an AI tool that drafts and submits them work?

Beliefs and verdicts:
- B1 Staff would let AI draft claims if a person approves each one: Not enough evidence (checked online only, not with real people)
- B2 Small practices would pay $300 a month: Not enough evidence (checked online only, not with real people)
- B3 Current practice software leaves claim drafting to staff: Not enough evidence
- B4 Practices lose meaningful staff time to claims each week: Not enough evidence
- B5 A new app can send claims through the companies that pass them to insurers: Supported

What this could mean:
1. Keep building the full draft and submit tool. Sending claims is possible today, but similar AI tools exist and trust and price are unknown.
2. Narrow the tool to the claim work staff still do by hand, such as written explanations and X-ray attachments. Some competitors already offer this.
3. Test further before deciding. Talk to practice staff about their last claims, the time spent, today's costs and the tools they have tried.

Main limits:
- Nobody has measured how many hours a week small practices spend on claims.
- No real people were asked, so trust and price are still open.
- Most price figures come from companies that sell billing services.

Choosing between these is a judgement call about your product: your goals, your users and what you're willing to trade off. It's often worth talking it through with someone who has made calls like this before.
